The Snake’s Promise

My MA final major project: a 72-second hand-drawn film that retells one Tamil couplet, around 2,000 years old, in an Indian folk-art style.

The Thirukkural has 1,330 couplets of just seven words each. I chose Kural 824, which warns you to fear people who smile sweetly while their heart is bitter. In my film, a man helps a snake that promises it won’t bite him. Every frame is hand-drawn in Clip Studio, and I voiced it myself. It was my first time doing both animation and voice.

“’Tis fitting you should dread dissemblers’ guile, Whose hearts are bitter while their faces smile.”

Watch the film Opens on YouTube

Role Story, art direction, animation, voice Tools Clip Studio Paint · Photoshop · Illustrator · After Effects · Premiere Pro Film 72 seconds · 18 shots · Kural 824, translated by G. U. Pope

Research & design

Temple murals, folk painting and a lot of drawing before the final look.

I started with storytelling: how games like Gris and A Plague Tale make you feel something without explaining it. Then I looked back home, at Sangam poetry, the murals at the Brihadeeswarar temple in Thanjavur, and Chola bronzes. I studied Madhubani, Gond, Warli and Kalamkari, and built my style from them: bold double outlines, flat colour, dots and no empty space. Madhubani gave me the eyes and patterns. My colour palettes come from temple stone, bronze, earth and textiles. My first character didn’t feel like folk art yet, so I kept drawing until he did. The story changed too: my first storyboard had a house, so I moved everything into the forest.

Research first, then draw, then redraw.

Sketch to film

A 77-second pencil animatic, trimmed to the 72-second film, then every shot redrawn in colour.

The animatic set the timing, and most of its shots made it into the film. My storyboard felt flat, so I took inspiration from anime. I started with seven camera angles and ended up with 18, cutting between the man’s worried face and the snake’s eye. The bite is an impact frame, which my professors singled out in my final presentation. I painted every asset in the same folk style: bushes, leaves, clouds, ground, stones and trees. Then I added sound, the kural read in Tamil and English subtitles. You feel the betrayal first, and the kural explains it at the end.

Next: one kural, one film. I want to make this a series.

Invisible Strings

“What controls you?” A black-and-white animated short.

A university project about the illusion of freedom. Everyone is controlled by something, not just me. Even I am controlled by rules and money. A puppet breaks free of its strings and wanders around using its own free will. Then a light flashes, it looks at the buildings and at its own hands, and sees that everything is still connected by strings. Something pulls it back up, and the film ends by asking you directly: what controls you? I used surrealism and Gestalt theory to show the feeling. Made in After Effects, and the string music was played and recorded by me.

From sketch to screen

My professor liked the Gestalt ideas, the colour contrast and the story. I still think I can do better.
